The MPU is down to two causes: a dead module, or 3.3 V not reaching its VCC pin through a broken crimp or cold joint. A multimeter across VCC and GND at the module separates them in seconds — worth doing before buying a replacement, in case the fault is the wire rather than the part.

HC-SR501 settings

  • Two pots. One sets sensitivity, the other sets how long OUT stays HIGH after a trigger — often factory-set to tens of seconds. A long setting looks exactly like a sensor stuck on.
  • One jumper. Repeat trigger (H) is what this firmware expects; single trigger makes motion look like it stops during continuous movement.
  • Warm-up is real. The module needs roughly a minute after power-up before its output means anything, which is why PIR_WARMUP_MS exists.

Before you power it

The LED matrix can brown out the board. MATRIX_BRIGHTNESS is 255 and the firmware fills all 16 pixels white on intrusion. That is roughly 16 × 60 mA ≈ 960 mA on the 5 V rail — more than USB supplies and far more than the DevKit's VIN trace and regulator are meant to pass. Expect brownout resets. Drop MATRIX_BRIGHTNESS to about 60, or feed the matrix from a separate 5 V supply with its ground tied to the ESP32's ground.
That RGB LED is a bare 5 mm part, not a KY-016 module. A module has current-limiting resistors on its PCB; a bare LED does not, and tying its legs straight to GPIOs will overdrive both the LED and the pin. Put a resistor of roughly 220 Ω in each of the three colour legs, between the GPIO and the leg. The firmware drives the channels HIGH to light them, so it expects common cathode — longest leg to ground. If yours is common anode, the longest leg goes to 3V3 instead and every colour will come out inverted until setLED() in smart_cone.ino:38 is flipped.
  • GY-521 needs its onboard regulator to take 5 V. The diagram puts it on 3V3, which is always safe. Only move it to the 5 V bus if you see the small 3-pin part marked 662K next to VCC.
  • WS2812B logic is marginal at 3.3 V. A 5 V-powered strip wants roughly 3.5 V for a logic high. ESP32's 3.3 V usually works on short leads; if pixels glitch, that is the reason.
  • Keep TX0 and RX0 clear. They carry the serial console. Anything wired there fights the flashing and monitoring path.
  • Verify before the full build. Wire only the MPU6050 first and confirm the I2C scan reports FOUND 0x68. Add the other five modules after that passes.
