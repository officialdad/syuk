Smart Cone — breadboard wiring

ESP32 DevKit 30-pin. Pin assignments taken from firmware/smart_cone/config.h.

Every jumper

One line per physical wire — nineteen of them. Power and ground included, because leaving them implicit is how a module ends up looking dead when only its supply is missing.

D14 and D27 are easy to swap and the symptoms mislead. The matrix reads pixel data on D14; the PIR reports motion on D27. Putting matrix DIN on D27 leaves the matrix dark and makes the PIR fire continuously, because pixel data on the input pin reads as endless motion. Two separate faults, one wrong hole.

Pin locations on the board

Every wire leaves a component from the edge facing the board — nothing wraps around the back. The supply bars sit in the gap between the components and the ESP32, so a power wire is a short stub, not a detour.

Before you power it

VIN must never touch a GPIO. It sits at about 4.7 V and ESP32 inputs are 3.3 V tolerant only. Fine on the VCC pad of the PIR or the matrix; damaging on any signal pin. This is the one wiring mistake that breaks the board rather than merely failing.
That RGB LED is a bare 5 mm part, not a module. It has no current-limiting resistors, so each of the three colour legs needs roughly 220 Ω between it and the GPIO. The firmware drives channels HIGH to light them, so it expects common cathode — longest leg to ground.
The matrix can brown out the board. All 16 pixels white at full brightness is about 960 mA on the 5 V rail, more than USB supplies. MATRIX_BRIGHTNESS is 60 for that reason; raise it only with a separate 5 V supply, its ground tied back to the ESP32's.
